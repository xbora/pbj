# PBJ Show Playbook

Source included from Bora's `ai_pub_chat_youtube_playbook.md`.

## Core Premise

PBJ should feel like two smart friends talking at a pub: fast enough for
YouTube, smart enough for professionals, funny enough for casual viewers, and
structured enough to generate Shorts.

The target feeling:

> I learned something useful while feeling like I was hanging out with
> entertaining people.

## Strategic Rule

The show is not really about news. News is the excuse.

The product is:

- chemistry
- reactions
- tension
- humor
- trust
- perspective

Most AI news content fails because it is too technical, too corporate, too
summary-driven, and emotionally flat. PBJ should favor clear emotional framing:
threat, opportunity, competition, weirdness, and human consequences.

## Episode Shape

- Cold open: 0:00-0:45. Start with the strongest disagreement, craziest story,
  or clearest emotional reaction. No slow intros.
- Fast intro: 0:45-1:05. Show name, what is coming, biggest tease.
- Main story: 1:05-10:00. Biggest implication first.
- Pattern interrupt: 10-15 minutes. Quick game, spicy prediction, roast,
  "bullish or bearish", or weird demo reaction.
- Secondary stories: 15-35 minutes. 4-7 minute chunks with visible chapter
  titles and recurring segment names.
- Strong ending: final 2-3 minutes. Bold prediction, audience question, or
  next-week tease.

## Host Dynamic

Best recurring tension for Paddy and Bora:

- Operator vs. Nerd: Paddy owns business, marketing, partner relationships,
  and operational reality. Bora owns technical systems, agents, MCP, and
  product/infrastructure takes.
- Builder vs. Skeptic: one host gets excited about the agentic future; the
  other asks whether it actually survives contact with clients, budgets, and
  messy workflows.
- Chaos vs. Calm: let one person push the spicy version and the other translate
  it back into useful operator language.

## Retention Rule

Every 30-60 seconds, something should change:

- joke
- disagreement
- surprising fact
- emotional reaction
- visual change
- story
- analogy
- prediction

## Story Ranking

Choose 3-5 stories well. Score candidates by:

- emotional reaction
- disagreement potential
- audience impact
- visual potential
- clip potential
- money, jobs, behavior, power, or culture angle

## Segment Prompts

Every story should answer:

- What happened?
- Why should normal people care?
- Who wins?
- Who loses?
- What job, workflow, or behavior changes?
- What is the strongest take?
- Where should Paddy push back?
- Where should Bora get technical?
- What is the clip?

## Recurring Segment Names

- Bullish or Bearish
- AI Tool We'd Actually Use
- Most Important AI Story Nobody Saw
- Big Tech Panic Meter
- This Will Replace a Job
- Dumbest Launch of the Week
- What This Means for Normal People

## Shorts Rules

Every episode should be treated as a Shorts factory.

Great Shorts need one idea, one emotional hook, and a fast payoff. Best formats:

- hot take
- debate
- prediction
- reaction
- explainer

Hook examples:

- "This AI launch is terrifying."
- "AI agents are about to break SaaS."
- "I think this kills half the tools people are building."

## Packaging

Titles should use:

> Big entity + unexpected consequence

Examples:

- "This OpenAI Launch Changes Ecommerce"
- "AI Agents Are About to Break SaaS"
- "Google Just Flipped AI Shopping Upside Down"

Thumbnails should communicate tension, consequence, and surprise. Avoid generic
"Weekly AI News" packaging.

## Production Notes

- Visual change every 3-8 seconds in the edit.
- Use screenshots, product UI, headlines, tweets/posts, graphs, demos, and
  reaction cams.
- Remove dead air aggressively.
- Start segments mid-energy.
- End transitions with tiny cliffhangers.
- Capture separate audio tracks.
