# YouTube Thumbnail Skill

Use this skill to create thumbnails that earn qualified clicks: clear enough to
understand in a feed, interesting enough to stop the scroll, and honest enough
that viewers keep watching after they click.

## Research Basis

Use these findings as current operating rules:

- YouTube recommends custom thumbnails use a 16:9 aspect ratio, JPG/GIF/PNG,
  minimum width of 640 px, and high resolution. Current YouTube Help lists
  3840 x 2160 px as the recommended custom thumbnail resolution, with upload
  limits depending on device. Source: YouTube Help, "Add custom thumbnails on
  YouTube" (https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72431).
- YouTube says thumbnails and titles should help viewers understand the video,
  target the right audience, use rule-of-thirds style composition, use readable
  text, avoid over-complexity, and be measured using CTR by traffic source in
  the first 24 hours. Source: YouTube Help, "Thumbnail & title tips"
  (https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12340300).
- YouTube's native A/B tool can test up to three titles/thumbnails, takes a few
  days to two weeks, and selects winners by watch time share rather than CTR
  alone. Variants should be meaningfully different and at least 1280 x 720; if
  any test thumbnail is below 720p, all test thumbnails may be downscaled.
  Source: YouTube Help, "A/B test titles and thumbnails"
  (https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16391400).
- YouTube thumbnail policy prohibits misleading thumbnails and images that use
  pornographic, violent/gory, hateful, harmful, dangerous, vulgar, or shocking
  content in policy-violating ways. Source: YouTube Help, "Thumbnails policy"
  (https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9229980).
- Creator-tool guidance generally converges on one clear idea, one emotion,
  3-5 words of thumbnail text, strong small-screen contrast, a clear face or
  object when relevant, and reference thumbnails for style consistency. Treat
  this as heuristic, not law. Source: vidIQ Help, "Best Practices: Getting the
  Most Out of vidIQ Thumbnails"
  (https://support.vidiq.com/en/articles/13946970-best-practices-getting-the-most-out-of-vidiq-thumbnails).

## Core Workflow

Before making pixels, decide the packaging.

1. Extract the strongest concrete hook from the video: a weird object, a risky
   claim, a direct conflict, a surprising number, or a funny line.
2. Pick one viewer promise. The thumbnail should not explain the whole episode.
3. Pick one emotion: curiosity, disbelief, tension, delight, danger, or "wait,
   what?"
4. Pick one visual anchor: host face, product/object, chart, app UI, terminal,
   prop, or simple diagram.
5. Pair thumbnail and title so they add to each other. Do not repeat the full
   title inside the image.
6. Create three meaningfully different variants for important videos.
7. Review at small size before finalizing.

## PBJ Thumbnail Positioning

PBJ should feel specific, human, technical, and slightly pub-chaotic. Avoid
generic AI slop.

Prefer:

- Bora and Paddy faces with real expressions.
- Real episode artifacts: the Run MCP shirt, Claude/Claude Code, terminal panes,
  GitHub PRs, Robinhood chart, Shopify checkout, agent email inboxes, beach
  setup.
- A concrete phrase from the episode.
- Wry danger: "this is useful, but possibly insane."
- Visual proof that the topic happened.

Avoid:

- Generic robots, glowing brains, blue circuit boards, vague AI faces, random
  neon hands, and meaningless "future of work" imagery.
- Text that sounds like a LinkedIn webinar.
- Too many logos.
- Dense screenshots that become gray mush on mobile.
- Mimicking another creator's exact thumbnail style.

## Design Rules

### One Clear Focal Point

Use one dominant subject that can be identified at a glance:

- one host face
- both hosts in a clean split-screen
- one product/object
- one UI moment
- one diagram element

If there are more than three major visual elements, simplify.

### Text

Use 0-5 words. For PBJ, 2-4 words is usually ideal.

Good thumbnail text:

- "CLAUDE BOUGHT THIS"
- "SAAS IS DEAD?"
- "AGENT TRADES?"
- "RUN MCP"
- "PRETTY GOOD IS FREE"
- "CLAUDE AT THE BEACH"

Bad thumbnail text:

- repeats the full title
- explains the whole episode
- uses tiny supporting copy
- includes jargon with no visual payoff

Use a bold sans-serif font, strong outline or shadow, and very high contrast.
Do not place text in the bottom-right corner, where YouTube's duration badge
will cover it.

### Faces

For conversational episodes, faces are usually useful because they signal human
stakes. Use close crops with visible emotion. Neutral faces are acceptable only
when the object or claim is doing the emotional work.

Good expressions:

- confused but amused
- skeptical
- surprised
- "are we really doing this?"
- laughing at the absurdity

Avoid fake shock if the episode tone is not actually shocking.

### Color And Contrast

Design for the feed, not for full-screen inspection.

- Make the subject brighter or darker than the background.
- Use saturated accent colors sparingly.
- Keep the background simple or blurred.
- Use color separation between text, subject, and background.
- Test in grayscale; if it becomes unreadable, the value contrast is too weak.

### Mobile And TV Test

Every thumbnail must pass:

- 128 px wide: can identify the subject and read any text.
- 320 px wide: composition still feels intentional.
- Full size: no ugly artifacts, weird hands, malformed text, or distracting
  details.

## PBJ Thumbnail Formulas

### 1. Concrete Artifact

Use when the episode has a real object or demo.

Structure:

- Host face on one side.
- Artifact on the other.
- 2-4 word text.

Examples:

- Bora holding/pointing at Run MCP shirt + text: "CLAUDE BOUGHT THIS"
- Claude/Shopify checkout UI + text: "AGENT SHOPPING"
- GitHub PR queue + text: "AGENTS AT WORK"

### 2. Dangerous Button

Use when the story involves money, permissions, automation, or risk.

Structure:

- Big UI/action visual.
- Worried or amused face.
- Red/yellow accent.
- Question text.

Examples:

- Robinhood chart + robot cursor + text: "AGENT TRADES?"
- Credit card + Claude logo-like abstract mark + text: "WHO APPROVED THIS?"
- Deploy button + terminal + text: "AUTO MERGE?"

### 3. Debate Split

Use when Paddy and Bora disagree or represent different angles.

Structure:

- Paddy on one side, Bora on the other.
- Object/claim in the middle.
- Short question.

Examples:

- "SAAS IS DEAD?"
- "BUILD OR BUY?"
- "AGENTS OR HUMANS?"

### 4. Simple Metaphor

Use when the episode's strongest line is abstract.

Structure:

- One metaphor image.
- One host reaction.
- Minimal text.

Examples:

- Ferrari steering wheel + text: "CAN YOU DRIVE IT?"
- Empty office chair + laptop + text: "AI COWORKER?"
- Beach chair + terminal window + text: "CLAUDE AT BEACH"

### 5. Founder Operator

Use when the episode is aimed at founders, agencies, or ecommerce operators.

Structure:

- Human face.
- Business artifact: dashboard, client report, Shopify store, Slack, inbox.
- Text with commercial consequence.

Examples:

- "PRETTY GOOD IS FREE"
- "YOUR AGENT TEAM"
- "NO MORE SAAS?"

## Prompt Template

When generating a thumbnail image, use this structure:

```text
Create a 16:9 YouTube thumbnail for a conversational AI/tech podcast episode.
Main promise: [one sentence].
Emotion: [one emotion].
Composition: [host face/object/text placement].
Visual anchor: [specific object, UI, chart, prop, or scene].
Text on image: "[0-5 words]".
Style: sharp, high-contrast, clean background, readable at mobile size, premium
tech-podcast energy, human and slightly playful, no generic robot/circuit-board
imagery, no tiny text, no misleading elements.
```

For PBJ, add:

```text
Tone should feel like two smart friends in a pub arguing about AI, not a
corporate webinar.
```

## Variant Strategy

For important episodes, create three A/B variants:

1. Curiosity variant: weird concrete object or unexpected line.
2. Risk variant: what could go wrong, money/job/power angle.
3. Human debate variant: Paddy vs. Bora or skeptical vs. excited expression.

Make variants meaningfully different. Do not test three nearly identical
versions with only slight color or text changes.

## Review Rubric

Score each category 0-3. A publishable thumbnail should score at least 18/24
and have no policy or truthfulness failure.

- Clear promise: viewer can infer the subject quickly.
- Curiosity: there is a reason to click.
- Truthfulness: the thumbnail matches the video.
- Mobile readability: subject and text work at 128 px wide.
- Contrast: subject, text, and background separate clearly.
- Emotional specificity: the face or object expresses a mood.
- Title pairing: thumbnail adds something the title does not already say.
- Simplicity: no clutter, no competing focal points.

## Final Checklist

Before delivering:

- Canvas is 16:9.
- Export is 3840 x 2160 preferred or 1280 x 720 minimum.
- File is JPG or PNG.
- If uploading from mobile, keep video thumbnails under 2 MB.
- If uploading from desktop, stay within YouTube's current desktop limit.
- For A/B tests, all variants are at least 1280 x 720.
- No text or key subject is hidden by the bottom-right timestamp.
- It passes 128 px and 320 px preview checks.
- It does not violate YouTube thumbnail policy.
- It does not promise something the video does not deliver.
- It uses real episode-specific assets whenever possible.

## Output Format

When asked for thumbnail help, return:

1. One recommended thumbnail concept.
2. Two A/B variants.
3. Exact text to put on the thumbnail.
4. Image-generation prompt or designer brief.
5. Short rationale tied to the episode's hook.
6. Any policy, truthfulness, or asset warnings.
